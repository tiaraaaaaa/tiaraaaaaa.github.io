GroundedReach: Enabling Body-grounded Haptic Experience in Virtual Reality with an Elbow Wearable Haptic Device

Yilong Lin, Tianze Xie, Yuxin Ma, Daniele Giunchi, Mike J. Sinclair, Seungwoo Je, Eyal Ofek

IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics (TVCG), Special Issue on ISMAR 2026 Paper

Haptics Virtual Reality

Abstract

Grounded haptic systems can make virtual objects feel resistant and solid, but conventional grounded devices and exoskeletons are often bulky, restrictive, and difficult to share. GroundedReach explores a more compact approach for common virtual reality scenarios in which a user remains seated or standing in one location while reaching toward virtual objects.

The elbow-worn device uses a single-joint architecture that combines brake-based dynamic passive haptic feedback with a servo-controlled ratchet, allowing it to render both continuous resistance and rigid stopping forces during arm extension and flexion. A pre-study found that elbow-only resistance was as effective as combined elbow-and-shoulder resistance for perceived realism, while two subsequent studies showed improvements in task efficiency, realism, and enjoyment over visual-only feedback.
