The Effect of Building Typology and Visual Complexity on Stress Levels through Amplitude Spectrum Analysis

Mirna Zordan, Piet J. H. Maree, Zixian Lei, Tianze Xie, Seungwoo Je

Building Research & Information DOI

Virtual Reality

Abstract

Building typology shapes the visual structure of streetscapes and may influence physiological stress responses. This study presents an integrative framework combining amplitude spectrum slope (-α) analysis, immersive Virtual Reality (VR), and Heart Rate Variability (HRV), measured using the standard deviation of normal-to-normal intervals (SDNN), to investigate how scene-level Spatial Frequency (SF) structure relates to stress.

We reconstructed immersive 360° VR streetscapes representing three common Hong Kong building typologies: cantilevered buildings, super high-rise podium-towers, and podium-towers. Amplitude spectrum slopes were calculated from the video frames, while participants’ physiological stress responses were assessed through SDNN during three five-minute exposure periods.

The results revealed significant differences in both α values and SDNN among the three typologies. The podium-tower streetscape produced the lowest mean α value (1.1758) and the highest mean SDNN (54.39), indicating comparatively lower physiological stress. Its α value was also the closest to 1, reflecting a spatial-frequency pattern more consistent with the statistical regularity commonly found in natural scenes. These findings demonstrate how visual analysis, physiological measurement, and immersive simulation can be combined to support evidence-based design for healthier high-density urban environments.
